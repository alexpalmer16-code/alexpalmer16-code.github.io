# Alex Palmer: writing guide

## Scope and audience

Apply this guide to website articles, decks, cards, descriptions and derivatives
for LinkedIn, X and an optional newsletter. The primary reader is a sales or GTM
leader deciding how AI should change commercial work. Founders and AI builders
are secondary readers, not a reason to make every piece speak to everyone.

This is the project's voice source of truth. Use `EDITORIAL.md` for subjects,
formats, evidence and the quality gate. Use `PUBLISHING.md` for release steps.

## Voice

- Use Australian English, concrete language and natural contractions.
- Be direct, practical, warm and confident. State the point early.
- Sound like an operator explaining a decision to another operator.
- Preserve Alex's judgment and uncertainty. Do not manufacture certainty,
  enthusiasm, bluntness or a polished persona.
- Use complete sentences and varied paragraph lengths. Keep genuine spoken
  phrasing without copying filler, transcription errors or rushed fragments.
- Cut corporate filler, generic AI commentary and unsupported superlatives.

## Zero em dashes

Alex's explicit preference is zero em dashes (Unicode U+2014). This overrides
earlier project guidance or saved voice preferences allowing dashes.

Rewrite the sentence using a full stop, comma, conjunction or parentheses.
Do not substitute double hyphens or spaced en dashes as a visual workaround.
Normal compound-word hyphens and numeric ranges remain useful.
The rule covers titles, body text, summaries, image text and channel versions.
Do not change a source quotation silently. If it contains an em dash, choose a
different accurate excerpt or paraphrase with attribution instead.

## Sentence patterns to remove

Treat these as editing checks, not as a claim that punctuation can identify AI:

- Repeated "not X, but Y" or "it isn't X; it's Y" constructions.
- Repetitive binary contrasts that flatten a real tradeoff.
- Stacks of short dramatic fragments, one-line slogans or repeated paragraph openings.
- Forced three-item lists, identical section rhythms and a numbered framework
  imposed on an argument that does not need one.
- Empty hooks such as "in today's rapidly evolving landscape" and stock
  transitions such as "here's the thing", "let that sink in" or "the future is here".
- Unexplained claims that something is game-changing, revolutionary or transformative.
- Excessive headings, bold emphasis, colons and artificial rhetorical questions.
- Engagement bait and conclusions that merely rephrase the introduction.

Keep a contrast only when it carries a precise argument. For article one, keep
the central line: "The unit of adoption is not the employee. It is the workflow."
Rewrite surrounding contrasts so that line is not one instance of a repeated device.

Explain causes and decisions directly. For example, a sentence about managers
requiring sellers to verify sources is more useful than a slogan about moving
from adoption to impact. Specificity must come from supplied evidence or from
an explicitly illustrative example, never an invented success story.

## The Alex test

Before drafting, identify the observation, decision, tradeoff or prediction that
Alex has supplied and is prepared to defend. Ask for missing inputs using the
contract in `EDITORIAL.md`. Do not assume that liking a sample anecdote confirms
it happened to him. A firsthand story is useful, but it is not mandatory for
every format if Alex's own defensible judgment is clear.

Retain distinctive approved lines rather than treating a whole published article
as a perfect voice model. Keep private writing samples and interview transcripts
outside GitHub unless Alex explicitly approves their publication.

## Worked workflows

Where a workflow makes the argument concrete, show its trigger, inputs, AI
contribution, human judgment, manager inspection, intended outcome and failure
risks. Use a compact table or prose; a diagram is optional.

Label an untested workflow "Illustrative workflow". Describe its outcome as
intended, not achieved. A method that sounds plausible does not prove business
impact. A real experiment needs a baseline, observed results and limitations.

## Structure and endings

Let the argument determine the structure. Useful elements include a genuine
observation, thesis, evidence, counterargument, example and decision. Do not
force every article to contain the same number of sections or a personal opener.

End with a concrete decision, a practical first action, a return to the opening
observation or a prediction with conditions. Use a question only if an answer
would advance the discussion. Do not automatically append one to every piece.

## Voice interview

Ask one short question and wait. Follow the answer rather than reading a list.
Aim for 10 to 15 minutes when evidence is already available. Establish:

1. What actually happened and what Alex noticed.
2. Which management routine or commercial constraint mattered.
3. What evidence supports the interpretation, and what else might explain it.
4. The decision Alex would make, its tradeoff and the reader's first action.

Do not draft while answers are still being collected. At the end, read back the
proposed thesis, usable evidence, uncertainty and privacy boundaries for approval.
If asked to revise article one, the account-planning workflow in the supplied
feedback is a candidate illustrative example, not a confirmed field result.

## Final edit

- Can Alex point to the part that reflects his own thinking?
- Does every factual claim have the right evidence or qualification?
- Is a worked example specific, useful and labelled honestly?
- Does the writing sound natural when read aloud?
- Can a paragraph be removed without weakening the argument?
- Are em dashes absent, including encoded HTML forms?
- Have repetitive contrasts, generic openings and manufactured cadence been removed?
- Does the ending give this reader a useful next step?

For a substantial revision, include a short private handoff: personal material
used, example status, missing research, voice issues fixed and unresolved choices.
Do not label a draft "AI-free" or rely on an AI-detector score as a quality test.
